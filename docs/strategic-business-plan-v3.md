# Strategic Business Plan v3: Uvalu.app
*The full-time variant — same product, same solo+AI model, a real income to replace.*

---

## Relationship to v1 and v2

This is not a replacement for [strategic-business-plan-v2.md](strategic-business-plan-v2.md) — it's the
**escalation path** you switch into deliberately, if and when the evidence says it's worth going full-time.
v2 stays the default, current mode: personal use, free time, zero pressure. v3 only activates if v2's own
milestone ("someone unprompted asks to pay," organic pull beyond invite-only) starts showing up for real.

[strategic-business-plan.md](strategic-business-plan.md) (v1) is still useful here in a way it wasn't for
v2 — its market analysis, competitive landscape, and GTM channel list were built for a scale v2 deliberately
ignored. v3 borrows from v1 selectively, resized for one person instead of a 10-person team.

**The honest answer to "can I make this a good business":** plausibly yes, as a solo niche SaaS — realistically
a strong full-time income for one person, not a venture-scale company. The product risk is already mostly
retired (see v2 [Section 1](strategic-business-plan-v2.md#1-what-we-actually-built-last-40-days) — 1,273
tests, a real auth system, a working multi-model valuation engine, all live). **The actual unknown is
customer acquisition** — v2 deliberately built zero muscle for it, so this plan's biggest open question isn't
"can it be built," it's "will ~600+ people actually pay for it," and that can only be answered by trying, not
by planning harder.

## 1. The Target, in Numbers

You set the target: **€4,000-6,000/month net personal income**, replacing a full-time job. This plan uses the
midpoint, **€5,000/month net**, as the planning figure — swap in your real number any time.

| Step | Figure | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Net income needed | €5,000/month | Your stated range's midpoint |
| Gross revenue needed | **~€9,000/month MRR** | Rough Belgian small-BV gross-up for tax + social contributions + basic business costs — **get the real multiplier from an accountant**, this is a planning placeholder, not a tax calculation |
| Blended ARPU | ~€15/month | Reused from v1's Pro (€9.99) / Premium (€24.99) pricing, weighted toward Pro |
| **Paying users needed** | **~600** | €9,000 ÷ €15 |
| Free→paid conversion assumption | ~3% (typical freemium range: 2-5%) | Planning assumption, not measured yet — v2 has never tried to convert anyone |
| **Total registered users needed** | **~20,000** | 600 ÷ 3% |

That "~20,000 registered users, ~600 paying" is the real-world size of a "good business" here — not v1's
200,000-user fantasy, and a genuine step up from v2's "a few dozen invited friends." Getting there is a
marketing and retention problem, not a product problem.

## 2. Executive Summary

Uvalu.app, built solo with an AI pair-programmer, becomes a full-time, bootstrapped, single-operator SaaS
business — still no co-founder, still no outside capital, but now with real marketing effort, a real
transition plan off a day job, and a real revenue target sized to replace an income rather than cover a
hosting bill.

### What changes from v2
| Dimension | v2 (current) | v3 (this plan) |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 5-10 hrs/week, free time | Full-time, this becomes the job |
| Users targeted | You + a small invited circle | ~20,000 registered / ~600 paying |
| Revenue goal | None required; a bonus if it happens | ~€9,000/month MRR, replacing €5,000/month net income |
| GTM | None (word of mouth only) | Real: content/SEO, a public launch, possibly modest paid acquisition funded by revenue |
| Legal structure | Unincorporated (bijberoep) | Incorporate (Belgian BV) at the transition point |
| Funding | Hosting costs only | Still fully bootstrapped — no external capital, per your answer |
| Team | You + AI, no hires ever | You + AI; a first hire only once it pays for itself |
| Regulatory posture | Informal, invite-only, low exposure | MiFID II investment-advice classification needs a real legal answer before a public launch |
| Risk tolerance | Zero financial risk | Managed risk — see [Section 3](#3-the-transition-plan--staying-low-risk-on-the-way-to-full-time) for how the jump itself stays low-risk |

### The structural advantage worth naming
Most solo SaaS founders eventually have to hire an engineer as the product grows, which is usually what forces
either raising money or capping ambition. You've already shown (v2 Section 1: 9 releases in ~2 weeks) that
AI-pair-programming substitutes for that hire. The constraint on reaching €9,000 MRR isn't going to be "can
the product keep up" — it's acquisition and retention, which is where this plan's effort should concentrate.

## 3. The Transition Plan — Staying Low-Risk on the Way to Full-Time

The jump itself is the riskiest part of this plan — quitting an income before replacing it. Keep it
low-risk with hard, pre-committed gates instead of a leap of faith:

### Phase 0 — Prove demand (still employed, still v2 pace-ish)
Turn on v2's optional monetization tier for real. Don't quit anything yet. Goal: get from 0 to genuine
paying customers and see if the funnel assumptions in [Section 1](#1-the-target-in-numbers) are even in the
right ballpark.
- **Gate to move to Phase 1**: at least a few dozen real paying customers acquired with minimal marketing
  effort — evidence people beyond your invite circle want this enough to pay.

### Phase 1 — Build the acquisition engine (part-time, ramping)
Start the GTM work in [Section 5](#5-go-to-market--now-it-has-to-actually-work) around the day job. This is
the phase most likely to take longer than hoped — budget 6-18 months, not weeks.
- **Gate to move to Phase 2 (quit the day job)**: **both** of the following, not either:
  1. MRR ≥ **€6,500-7,000/month** (roughly 72-78% of the €9,000 target) sustained for **3+ consecutive
     months**, with a visible growth trend, not a plateau or decline.
  2. **6-12 months of personal living expenses saved**, untouched, as a runway buffer — this is what keeps
     "full-time" from becoming a financial risk if growth stalls right after you quit.
- **No-go rule**: if Phase 1 runs 18-24 months without a credible trajectory toward the MRR gate, don't
  quit — either recalibrate the income target down, extend the timeline, or fall back to v2 permanently.
  Staying at v2 is a legitimate outcome, not a failure.

### Phase 2 — Full-time
Quit, incorporate ([Section 6](#6-structure--team-legal-tools)), and put full-time hours into closing the
gap from ~€7,000 to the ~€9,000 MRR target, then sustaining and growing past it.

### Phase 3 — Beyond target
Optional. Once MRR comfortably clears target with margin, decide fresh whether to keep growing, hold steady,
or take the sustainable income and enjoy it — there's no obligation here to chase growth for its own sake,
consistent with v2's "fun and reversibility" philosophy carried forward.

## 4. Product Scope — What Changes From v2

Everything already live (screener, portfolio tracker, dashboard, 8-stage risk engine, multi-user auth,
encrypted backups, admin portal) stays as the foundation — no rebuild needed, it already works.

### Reconsidered from v2's "explicitly not planned" list
| Item | v2 stance | v3 stance |
|---|---|---|
| Public REST API | Not planned | Revisit only if power users show real willingness to pay a premium for it — evidence-gated, not upfront |
| Mobile app | Not planned | Revisit only after web PMF is proven (real paying users, real retention data) — expensive to build and maintain, don't front-load it |
| Paid market-data provider | Only if yfinance breaks | **Upgrade proactively once revenue justifies it** — at 600 paying customers, "the entire business depends on an unofficial free API" becomes a real single-point-of-failure risk, not a hypothetical |
| AI-insights / social features | Scope creep, dropped | Only if they measurably move conversion or retention — treat as a GTM/retention lever, not a "cool to have" |

### New requirements v2 never needed
- **A real, working payment flow** (Stripe or similar) with proper invoicing/receipts, not a one-off hack.
- **A support channel with an actual response-time expectation** — email is fine at this scale, but it needs
  to be answered, unlike v2's community-Discord-is-enough posture.
- **EU VAT/OSS compliance** for cross-border B2C billing — real once there are real paying customers in
  multiple countries.
- **A public-facing "not investment advice" disclaimer, reviewed properly** — see
  [Section 8](#8-risk-management).

## 5. Go-to-Market — Now It Has to Actually Work

v2's "no GTM, word of mouth only" doesn't get to 20,000 registered users. This borrows from v1's channel list,
resized to what one person can actually execute:

### Organic (do this first — it's free, just slow)
- **SEO content**: the same targets v1 identified are still valid ("best stock screener Europe," "undervalued
  European stocks") — a weekly or biweekly blog post is realistic solo output, not v1's implied full content
  team.
- **A public launch post** (Show HN, r/EUinvesting-style communities, a value-investing forum you're already
  part of) — one well-prepared post, not an ongoing campaign.
- **Build in public**: given the real story here (solo + AI shipping 9 releases in two weeks), that story
  itself is marketing — a build log, changelog-as-content, or a newsletter documenting the journey can
  attract exactly the audience (technically curious value investors) this product is for.

### Paid (only once organic proves the funnel, and only self-funded)
- Small, capped experiments (e.g. €100-300/month in search ads) funded from existing MRR, never from savings
  — if it doesn't pay for itself within a couple of months, stop.
- No large ad budget, ever, at solo scale — v1's CAC/LTV machinery only starts mattering past this point, and
  even then, keep it simple: track one number (cost per paying customer) against ARPU, nothing fancier.

### Partnerships (opportunistic, not a program)
A single broker/blog partnership is plausible for one person to negotiate directly, if the right relationship
exists — but this plan doesn't depend on it. Treat it as upside, not a channel to build a process around.

### What stays dropped from v1
No sales team, no dedicated growth hire (until Phase 3, if ever), no LinkedIn thought-leadership program, no
multi-channel paid campaign — all of that assumes people-hours this plan doesn't have.

## 6. Structure — Team, Legal, Tools

### Team
Still solo + AI through Phase 2. The AI-pair-programming advantage from
[Section 2](#2-executive-summary) means the first hire, if it ever happens, should go toward **acquisition or
support work** (the actual bottleneck), not engineering — that's a meaningful difference from a typical solo
SaaS founder's hiring order.
- **First-hire trigger** (Phase 3 only): MRR comfortably exceeds target with margin, and a specific
  time-consuming task (support volume, content production) is the visible bottleneck to further growth.

### Legal structure
**Incorporate at the Phase 1→2 gate**, not before. This is exactly the trigger v2's Section 3 already named
("recurring paid revenue... or committing full time"). A Belgian BV at this income level is worth the
overhead it wasn't worth at v2 scale — liability protection matters once this is the sole income source, and
there are real tax-efficiency reasons to structure director's pay + dividends properly.
- Realistic incorporation cost: **~€1,500-2,500** (online formation service + a few hours of a lawyer/notary
  as required), not v1's fictional €10,000 legal-setup line.
- Ongoing: an accountant for BV bookkeeping/filings, realistically **~€1,500-3,000/year** at this scale.

### Tools
Add only what solo operation at this scale genuinely needs: a payment processor (Stripe), a simple
support/helpdesk (a shared inbox is enough at first), basic analytics to see the funnel numbers in
[Section 1](#1-the-target-in-numbers) for real. Still no project-management SaaS stack — that's overhead for
a team that doesn't exist.

## 7. Financial Plan

### One-time costs (at the Phase 1→2 transition)
| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| BV incorporation | €1,500-2,500 |
| Legal review of disclaimers/ToS (MiFID II posture) | €500-1,500 |
| **Total transition cost** | **~€2,000-4,000** |

### Ongoing monthly costs (Phase 2, illustrative)
| Item | Cost/month |
|---|---|
| Hosting (scaled up from v2's VPS, still modest at ~600 paying / ~20K registered users) | €50-150 |
| Paid market-data API (if adopted) | €100-300 |
| Payment processing fees (~2.5-3% of revenue) | ~€225-270 at €9K MRR |
| Accounting | ~€150-250 |
| Support/helpdesk tooling | €0-50 |
| Marketing (organic-first; paid only if self-funding, see Section 5) | €0-300 |
| **Total realistic monthly overhead** | **~€500-1,300** |

At the €9,000/month MRR target, overhead of ~€500-1,300 still leaves a large margin for the founder's own
gross-to-net gap ([Section 1](#1-the-target-in-numbers)) — this isn't a thin-margin business at this scale.

### Revenue staging (illustrative, not a commitment)
| Milestone | Paying users | MRR | Phase |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demand proven | ~30-50 | ~€500-750 | Phase 0 |
| Transition gate reached | ~430-470 | €6,500-7,000 | End of Phase 1 |
| Target reached | ~600 | €9,000 | Phase 2 |
| Comfortable margin | 800+ | €12,000+ | Phase 3 (optional) |

### Funding
**Fully bootstrapped**, per your answer — revenue and personal savings only, no angel/grant/friends-and-family
money. This means the pace through Phase 1 is genuinely gated by organic + self-funded-paid growth, not
accelerated by outside capital. That's a slower path than v1's funded version, but it means no equity given
up and no investor to answer to, ever.

## 8. Risk Management

Risks that are new or meaningfully bigger than v2's, now that this is a real income source:

| Risk | Why it's different from v2 | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **The acquisition funnel doesn't work** | This is the central unproven assumption in the whole plan | Phase 0/1 exist specifically to test this cheaply before quitting anything |
| **Quitting too early** | v2 had nothing to lose financially; this plan does | The hard, dual gate in [Section 3](#3-the-transition-plan--staying-low-risk-on-the-way-to-full-time) (sustained MRR **and** savings runway) exists specifically to prevent this |
| **MiFID II investment-advice classification** | v2 treated this as low-exposure (invite-only, personal use); at ~20,000 registered users with public marketing and real payments, this is a genuine open question that needs an actual answer, not a placeholder disclaimer | Get real legal review before the public launch in [Section 5](#5-go-to-market--now-it-has-to-actually-work) — budgeted in [Section 7](#7-financial-plan) |
| **yfinance dependency at real-business scale** | A free, unofficial API breaking is an annoyance for a personal tool; it's a business-continuity risk once 600 people are paying | Proactive paid-provider migration once revenue supports it (Section 4), rather than waiting for it to break |
| **Key-person risk** | You are the entire engineering, support, and product team — no redundancy | Document operational knowledge as you go (the codebase already has strong docs/tests); this is a real ceiling on how big this can get solo, and that's an accepted tradeoff, not a gap to panic about |
| **Burnout, now under real financial pressure** | Full-time removes the "it's just for fun, I can stop anytime" safety valve v2 had | The no-go rule in Phase 1 and the "fall back to v2" option in every phase keep an explicit, planned exit ramp — burnout isn't just a personal-wellness risk here, it's a business risk worth designing against |
| **Competition** | Real once actually marketing publicly (Morningstar, Simply Wall St, TradingView — see v1's competitive landscape) | Same differentiators v1 identified still hold (EU focus, transparent multi-model methodology, price) — nothing new needed here, just actually communicating them via Section 5 |

## 9. Decision Gates (Not a Fixed Roadmap)

The whole plan runs on gates, not dates — move to the next phase only when its gate is actually met:

- [ ] **Gate 0→1**: A few dozen real paying customers acquired with minimal marketing effort.
- [ ] **Gate 1→2 (quit the day job)**: MRR ≥ €6,500-7,000/month sustained 3+ months **and** 6-12 months of
      living expenses saved.
- [ ] **No-go check**: if 18-24 months into Phase 1 with no credible trajectory toward the gate — fall back
      to v2, recalibrate, or extend. This is a legitimate, planned outcome.
- [ ] **Phase 2→3 (first hire, optional)**: MRR comfortably clears €9,000 target with margin, and a specific
      bottleneck (support/content volume) is visibly limiting further growth.
- [ ] **Annual check-in**: re-read this plan and [v2](strategic-business-plan-v2.md) together; confirm the
      income target, the funding stance, and whether full-time is still the right call versus reverting to
      the free-time model.

## 10. What Success Looks Like

In order:

1. **The transition gate is met honestly** — real, sustained MRR plus real runway, not a hopeful guess.
2. **€5,000/month net income is replaced, sustainably**, without secretly working more hours than the old
   job for less security.
3. **It's still your product** — decisions on features, pricing, and pace stay yours; no investor,
   co-founder, or board changes that.
4. **It stays a business you'd choose again** — if full-time turns out to cost more (in stress, in fun) than
   it pays back, reverting to v2 is success too, not failure. The plan is explicitly designed to make that
   reversal cheap and shame-free.
5. **Optional stretch**: it grows past the target and eventually supports a small team — genuinely possible
   given the product's head start, but never the definition of success this plan is built around.
